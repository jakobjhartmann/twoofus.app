Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

This is a convenience translation. The legally binding version is the German privacy policy.

In short: TwoOfUs is built for two people. Since version 1.5, your shared content lives in the private iCloud database at Apple, specifically in the iCloud of whichever of you created the invitation. The other reaches the same data through an iCloud share. Only whoever redeems your invite code gets access; we ourselves have no access to your content. There are no servers of our own, no tracking, no advertising, and no account other than Sign in with Apple. This website sets no cookies and uses no trackers. You can delete your account in the app at any time; section 9 explains exactly what that deletes.

This privacy policy explains what personal data the iOS app TwoOfUs (“TwoOfUs,” “the app,” “we”) and the TwoOfUs website process, for what purpose, on what legal basis, and what rights you have. It is based on the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). It covers version 1.7 and the upcoming version 1.8; where the two versions differ, this is stated.

2. What data the app processes

TwoOfUs only processes what is needed for the shared features. Shared content is stored in a private iCloud database and shared with your partner (see section 6). Anything that stays only on your device is marked as such below:

  • Apple user identifier: a stable, app-specific ID from Sign in with Apple. It lets us recognize your account without ever processing an email address or password. It is stored in your profile record in the shared iCloud space so the app can match the two of you as a couple, which means your partner’s device can read it.
  • Profile name / initials: the display name you choose. It is shown to your partner and used for initials in the app and widgets.
  • Location (optional): the city, country, coordinates, and time zone of your location, used to calculate the distance for the Distance widget and to show the Partner Clock. You can pick a city from the list (its coordinates and time zone are then stored) or use your device location. Once you have granted the iOS permission, the app updates your location automatically when you move a significant distance and shares it with your partner; with the “Always” permission this also happens while the app is closed. Up to version 1.7, coordinates are stored and shared as precisely as iOS provides them. From version 1.8, they are rounded to about 1 km before they are stored or shared. Section 11 explains when the app asks for permission and how the city and time zone are determined.
  • Shared note and message history: a short note (up to 100 characters) shown in the Note widget. A new note triggers a notification to your partner. In addition, every note you send is stored with its sender and time as an entry in your shared message history. These entries remain until the shared data is deleted (see sections 7 and 9).
  • Countdowns: the target date and optional label of your shared countdown, plus any additional countdowns you create. Additional countdowns are stored on your device and also shared with your partner, who can see them but not change them.
  • Private countdown notes: notes and reminders you attach to a countdown are stored only locally on your device. They are not placed in the shared iCloud database and are not shared with your partner. The app schedules their reminders as local notifications on your device.
  • Streak counter: a counter of your active days or check-in weeks within the app.
  • Partner Clock: to display your partner’s local time, the app uses the time zone stored with their location and shows it along with their name and city.
  • Pairing code: a short code in the format XXXX-XXXX that your partner uses to join your invitation. Whoever redeems the code gets access to your shared data, so only share it with your partner. The code also serves as the identifier for the technical records that trigger notifications (see sections 6 and 12).
  • Date invitations (from version 1.6): the content and design of an invitation — the chosen idea, title, your card message, the date/time (in both time zones), the design of the flower bouquet, as well as replies, proposed times, and reactions.
  • Photos in date memories (from version 1.6): photos and optional notes that you add to a shared memory after a date. Access to your photo library only happens via the iOS photo picker, and only when you select photos yourself (see section 13).
  • Check-in answers (from version 1.6): your answers to the check-in questions and your reactions to your partner’s answers. When you submit, your answers are stored in the iCloud share like all other shared content and synced to your partner’s device. If the seal is on (the default), the app only shows your answers to your partner once you have both submitted, and vice versa. This is a display rule in the app, not encryption: the answers are already in the share and on your partner’s device before that.
  • Compliments (from version 1.6): sentences from your check-ins that you place in your shared compliment jar.
  • Technical metadata: an invite pointer and notification signal records in the app’s public CloudKit database. They contain no content (see section 6).

3. What we don’t collect

  • No email address and no password: sign-in runs entirely through Apple’s Sign in with Apple.
  • No payment or billing data.
  • No advertising IDs and no advertising.
  • No cross-app or cross-website tracking, no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs.
  • No cookies and no tracking on this website (see section 15).
  • We never sell or rent out data.

4. What we use the data for

We use the data listed above solely for the app’s core functions: to sign you in, connect you with your partner, and provide the shared features — the widgets (distance, countdown, note, partner clock), message history, date invitations and memories, weekly check-ins, and the compliment jar. Certain actions (e.g. a new note, a date invitation, or an acceptance) trigger a notification to your partner. We do not use your data for profiling, marketing, or any other purpose.

5. Legal bases (GDPR)

  • Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract): processing your account identifier, profile, pairing, note and message history, countdowns, streak, partner clock, date invitations, memories (including photos you select), check-in answers, and compliments, and sharing them with your partner, is necessary to provide the service you requested. Photo selection and calendar entries always happen only as a result of your own, active action.
  • Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (consent): your device location is only processed with your explicit consent via the iOS permission prompt. You can withdraw this consent at any time in iOS Settings.
  • Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interests): security and integrity of the service, abuse prevention, and the technically necessary delivery of this website.

6. Storage in iCloud and recipients

Private iCloud database: since version 1.5, your shared content is stored in a dedicated area of the private iCloud database (Apple CloudKit) of whichever of you created the invitation, and counts toward that person’s iCloud storage. Through an Apple-managed iCloud share, the other partner can read and edit this data. Whoever redeems your invite code gets access to the share. We run no servers and no database of our own, and we have no access to your content.

Couples from before version 1.5: couples who connected before version 1.5 and have not yet switched to the private iCloud still sync their profile (name, city, coordinates), note, countdown, and streak through records in the public CloudKit database that are found via your pairing code. The app offers these couples the switch.

Public CloudKit database (metadata without content): for two technical purposes, the app places small records in the app’s public CloudKit database, which is not private:

  • Invite pointer: while an invitation is open, a record named after the pairing code holds the link to your iCloud share. The inviter’s app deletes it once it notices that your partner has joined, when the invitation is cancelled, or on an app launch once it is older than 72 hours and has not been accepted. If your partner leaves the connection, the app creates the pointer again for the same code so you can reconnect.
  • Notification signal records: when you, for example, write a note, send a date invitation, or reply to one, the app updates a record containing your pairing code, your role in the pairing (inviter or invitee), the type of event, and the time. There is only one such record per device and event type, and it is overwritten with every event. When you unlink or delete your account, the app tries to remove this device’s records. Section 12 explains how these become notifications.

Recipient: your partner. Whoever is connected with you receives all shared content from section 2 on their device, including your profile name, your Apple user identifier, your city and country, your coordinates (up to version 1.7 at the precision iOS provides, from version 1.8 rounded to about 1 km), and your time zone. Your private countdown notes never leave your device.

Recipient: Apple. Apple Inc. provides the underlying infrastructure (iCloud/CloudKit, Sign in with Apple, push notifications, and the geocoding service that turns coordinates into a place name and time zone, see section 11). Apple’s privacy policy applies to these services: apple.com/privacy.

Replying via WhatsApp (optional): in a check-in, you can reply to one of your partner’s answers via WhatsApp. When you tap this, the app hands the quoted sentence to WhatsApp through a wa.me link; if WhatsApp is not installed, the wa.me page opens in your browser. The text then goes to WhatsApp (Meta), whose own privacy terms apply. Nothing is sent to WhatsApp unless you tap.

Transfers to third countries (USA): the provider of the infrastructure used is Apple Inc., headquartered in the USA; personal data may be transferred there as part of iCloud/CloudKit. The legal basis is the European Commission’s adequacy decision on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (to the extent the respective recipient is certified), supplemented by standard contractual clauses under Art. 46 GDPR. This website is likewise hosted via GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA); GitHub processes your IP address on the same legal basis (see section 15).

7. Retention period

We do not store data from the app ourselves, because we run no servers of our own. For data in iCloud and on your device:

  • Shared content (profile, location, note and message history, countdowns, invitations, memories and photos, check-in answers, compliments) stays in the inviter’s iCloud so both of you can see it, until it is deleted: individually in the app where that is offered, entirely via “Unlink and delete shared data” (inviter only), or when an account is deleted (see section 9).
  • After unlinking without deleting, the shared content stays in the inviter’s iCloud so you can reconnect later. The former partner’s profile record is removed as soon as the inviter creates a new invitation.
  • Invite pointer and signal records: as described in section 6.
  • Local data on your device (cached content, your countdowns, private countdown notes): until you delete your account in the app or remove the app from your device.
  • Apple’s own deletion and retention cycles apply to backups within the iCloud infrastructure.

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR, you have the right to:

  • access the data stored about you (Art. 15);
  • rectification of inaccurate data (Art. 16);
  • erasure of your data (Art. 17);
  • restriction of processing (Art. 18);
  • data portability (Art. 20);
  • object to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21);
  • withdraw any consent given, with effect for the future (Art. 7(3));
  • lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority (Art. 77).

To exercise these rights, write to us at jakobsworld@web.de. Because your content is stored in iCloud and not with us, we cannot view or delete it ourselves; section 9 describes the deletion options in the app and in iOS.

9. Deleting your account

You stay in control. In the app, you can delete your account under Settings → Account → “Delete Account”. This signs you out and resets the app data stored locally on this device, including your countdowns and private countdown notes. In iCloud, the following happens:

  • If you created the invitation (the shared data is in your iCloud): the app deletes the shared area in your iCloud together with all shared content, including what your partner contributed; your partner loses access. Up to version 1.7, this only happens while you are still connected. If you unlinked earlier, the data stays in your iCloud in version 1.7. If you want to unlink and delete everything in version 1.7, choose “Unlink and delete shared data” when unlinking; if you have already unlinked, delete the data in iOS (see below). From version 1.8, “Delete Account” always deletes the shared area in your iCloud, even after an earlier unlink.
  • If you joined with a code: your access to the share ends. Up to version 1.7, your profile record (name, Apple user identifier, city, coordinates, time zone) and the content you contributed remain in your partner’s iCloud. From version 1.8, the details in your profile record (name, initials, Apple user ID, city, country, coordinates, time zone) are deleted; shared content such as memories and check-in answers stays with your partner.

You can also delete data in your own iCloud at any time in iOS: Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Storage → TwoOfUs. You can additionally revoke the app’s access to Sign in with Apple under your Apple Account in iOS Settings. We have no access to data in your partner’s iCloud and therefore cannot delete it for you. If you have questions, jakobsworld@web.de is happy to help.

10. Children

TwoOfUs is not directed at children. The app is intended for people aged 13 and older. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. If a child has used the app, their data can be deleted in the app or in iOS as described in section 9; if you have questions about this, jakobsworld@web.de is happy to help.

11. Location permissions

The Distance widget needs your location to calculate how far apart you are. For your device location, iOS asks you for permission: in version 1.7 the first time the main screen opens, from version 1.8 only when you set up the distance. You can choose “While Using” or “Always”. “Always” enables background updates on significant location changes so the distance stays current even when the app is closed. Every new location is shared with your partner (see section 6).

To determine the city, country, and time zone from your coordinates, the app sends them to Apple’s geocoding service (MapKit or Core Location); Apple’s privacy policy applies to this. If the service returns no time zone, the app uses your device’s time zone.

You can change or revoke this permission at any time under iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → TwoOfUs. Without location access the app still works — only the distance feature is then turned off.

12. Notifications (push)

TwoOfUs sends you notifications so you don’t miss anything: for example when your partner writes a new note, sends you a date invitation, accepts an invitation, proposes a different time, adds photos to a memory, completes a check-in, or reminds you about the check-in.

We do not run a push server of our own. Your partner’s app updates one of the signal records from section 6, and Apple delivers a notification to you through the Apple Push Notification Service, based on a CloudKit subscription registered for your device. These notifications contain fixed text, not the content of your notes or answers. Apple also wakes the app with silent notifications when shared data has changed, so it can sync. Reminders for your weekly check-in and for countdown notes are scheduled locally on your device.

You can turn off notifications at any time under iOS Settings → Notifications → TwoOfUs.

13. Photos & photo library

For date memories, you can add photos. Access to your photo library happens exclusively via the iOS photo picker, and only when you actively select photos; the app does not read your library in the background. Selected photos are stored in your shared iCloud space (see section 6) and are visible to you and your partner.

14. Calendar access

Once you’ve arranged a date, you can add it to your iOS calendar with a tap. This access is write-only and happens only as a result of your explicit action (“Add to iOS Calendar”). TwoOfUs does not read your calendar. The added event then lives in your own calendar account and is subject to its settings.

15. Invitation links & this website

Invitation links: you can share a date invitation as a link (a link to the TwoOfUs website). The invitation data is encoded in the so-called fragment of the link — the part after the #. For technical reasons, browsers never send the fragment to a server: when your partner opens the link, the invitation is rendered purely locally in the browser. Neither we nor the website’s hosting provider ever receive the invitation’s content. After the date, the invitation page no longer shows the invitation, but the link itself still contains the data. Anyone who has the link can read its content, so only share it with your partner.

Website: this website is static and hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA). It sets no cookies, uses no trackers or analytics services, and does not load fonts, scripts, or other content from third-party servers. When you visit, GitHub processes your IP address for technical reasons in order to deliver the pages and, according to GitHub, logs it in server logs for security purposes. We have no access to these logs and do not analyze them; GitHub determines how long they are kept. For details, see GitHub’s privacy statement. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; our legitimate interest is the secure and reliable delivery of the website.

16. Security

The transfer between your device and Apple’s iCloud servers is encrypted; storage takes place within Apple’s secured infrastructure. Apple only makes your shared data available to the participants of your iCloud share. Whoever redeems your invite code becomes a participant, so treat the code like a password and only share it with your partner. We have no access to your content. The app only shows sealed check-in answers to your partner once both of you have submitted them; this is a display rule in the app, not additional encryption.
